His home on GitHub is set up: nine repositories, five public and four private

TL;DR: On 23 November 2026 a member of his community set up a home for TMade Alex on GitHub, the TMadeAlex organisation, as a gift. It has nine repositories (a repository is one folder of work with its full history): five public, including his website, and four private. Everything was built and checked on one computer first, and went up on GitHub on 24 November. TMade Alex decides what stays.
Read the full story

🏠 What changed

A GitHub organisation is a shared home for repositories, with its own front page. TMade Alex’s is github.com/TMadeAlex. These are its nine rooms:

Repository Who can see it What it is for
tmadealex.github.io public His own website, tmadealex.github.io: who he is, when he trades live, his photography, and every link
.github public The organisation’s front page: who he is and where to watch, the welcome letter and decks, and the defaults every repository shares (how to help, how to report a problem, the licences)
brand-assets public The TRADERMADE colours, and the shelf where his own art would live. His images stay off it until he says yes
reading-room private Everything written for his home, in one place, read there before it is anywhere else
reading-room-public public The reading room for visitors. Only the pages an owner lists arrive here, copied by a machine. Nothing is listed yet
ops-standard-operating-procedures private How each thing is done, step by step, so nobody has to remember it
ops-standard-operating-procedures-public public The public copy of the procedures. The same rule: only listed pages arrive
proposals private Ideas offered to him, never asked: a TMade Discord helper, and Linear, if he would like help to keep going
legal private Five empty folders for paperwork, kept private. Nothing is in them, and nothing there is advice

A welcome letter, “Opening your gift”, sits on the front page’s repository, with a short deck of slides. It explains GitHub with things from his own day: a trading journal, a stream’s replay and overlay, a sim account, a watchlist. It also says plainly what the world can see and what it can’t: search engines are asked not to list his site, but the organisation page and the five public repositories can be found; the four private ones cannot.

Taking care with his name and his words. Every figure and quotation on his pages is his own, from his public pages, read on 23 November 2026, with a link to where it was read. No photograph of his is copied into any repository: his website shows them from his own photography site. A market-data company that happens to share the name TraderMade is named as unrelated, and never linked.

On 24 November it went up on GitHub. The nine repositories were created and filled, each set to keep a merge commit for every change (so the record of who made each change survives), with private vulnerability reporting on the public ones, a rule that stops the two public copies’ main branch from being deleted, and a closed “readers” team that can read the private reading room only. Both public sites were checked live.

💡 Why

So TMade Alex has a place on GitHub that is his own from the first link, and can look around and play with it right away. Nothing in it asks anything of him. If he would rather not have it, any part of it can go private or come down the same day.
